Assumptions and limits

What the model does, what it assumes about your data, and what it has not been tested against.

This is an illustrative model. It has not been validated against real data, benchmarked against another tool, or reviewed by a practitioner. Do not use it for regulatory reporting or decisions without your own validation.

What is modelled

Each institution has a balance sheet that always balances: reserves, loans and interbank claims on one side; deposits, equity and interbank borrowing on the other. Every flow is a matched pair, so money and balance-sheet identities hold at every step. Four channels move stress between institutions: depositors withdrawing cash, institutions selling loans into a shared market whose price falls as it fills, interbank lending that moves liquidity from institutions with more to those with less, and defaults that pass an insolvent institution's shortfall to its creditors.

Settings you can change

Every setting has a default (the value the model has always used), a hard limit and an advisory band. The hard limit is the widest range where the model still works, and it is the only thing enforced: a share must be above 0% and at most 100%, rates and discounts cannot be negative, a discount cannot exceed 100%. The advisory band is roughly half to one and a half times the default, the region the model was explored over. Outside it the window says so and the run goes ahead. The bands are not a view of what is realistic. Anything you change is marked in the window, listed on the results page, and recorded in the run manifest (the options the model was rebuilt with, and the changes made to the scenario) when you export.

Four ways to treat the interbank network

  • Exposures as given (estimated from each institution's totals if none were given): the network the model has always used.
  • Estimated: each institution's interbank totals spread over the others in proportion, even where exposures were given.
  • Interbank frozen: every institution keeps its interbank claims and borrowing at book value, but no cash moves between institutions and no losses are passed on when one fails.
  • No interbank exposure: the interbank balances are removed. Each institution settles its net position (what it lent less what it borrowed) in cash, so its equity is unchanged and its balance sheet still balances. It cannot be used where an institution's net borrowing exceeds its cash, and the window says so.

The two ways of having no network are different things and can give different answers, so compare all four (on the Explore step) before relying on any one.

What exploring does and does not tell you

  • Ranking is the same shock on each institution in turn: how much damage a run on each would do, if it happened. It does not say which institution is likely to be run on. The model has no idea.
  • The breaking point is the first change found across the range you give, at a stated resolution, checked by running just below and just above it. It is not proven to be the only one: the window says when the outcome changes more than once, and the answer depends on how long the simulation runs.
  • Which assumptions matter halves and raises each assumption by half around its current value, one at a time. It ranks over ranges we chose, not which assumption is most uncertain, and it ignores interactions. Where a change alters who fails, the assumption is marked fragile in words. The reserve target is: on the sample data, halving it means nobody fails in the severe scenario.

What it simplifies

  • Losses pass to creditors in fixed shares, not through a solved clearing vector (Eisenberg–Noe).
  • Interbank loans have no maturities or repayment, and negative equity is not floored, so a negative number in the results is the shortfall left for creditors beyond the institution's capital, shown as such, and its size is illustrative.
  • There are no capital or liquidity regulations (ratios, buffers), no derivatives, no funding markets beyond interbank lending, and no behavioural response by institutions beyond the modelled channels.
  • Results depend on assumptions above that are not calibrated to your institutions. Which of them drives a result is listed under "What drives this result" beside every run.
  • Failure days come from a simulation that steps in tenths of a day and are good to about a day or two; the order in which institutions fail is what to rely on.
  • Who lent to whom decides how a run spreads. If you give no exposures file they are estimated from each institution's interbank totals, and an estimated network can spread stress very differently from the real one, so the window says so beside every result. Treat that case as one possible network.
  • Cash is limited by payment capacity, but there is no separate liquidity failure: an institution short of cash is shown by its lowest cash in the results table, and it fails in the results only when its equity turns negative.
